About

Conflict Watch Globe is an open-source intelligence (OSINT) visualisation project that maps wars, armed conflicts, mass protests and the persecution of minorities on a 3D satellite globe.

What it shows

The globe shades the provinces where ongoing conflicts take place. Selecting a marker opens an overview, duration, casualty figures, latest developments and related reporting. Zoomed in, you can see satellite imagery, terrain and place names, along with a situation simulation that scales with the size of the conflict. The simulation is a symbolic way to convey scale; it does not reproduce real troop positions or combat.

Data and methods

  • Curated events: 25 major conflicts are summarised by hand from public reporting by the UN, international organisations, news agencies and research institutes, with sources listed.
  • Automatic collection: Wikipedia’s List of ongoing armed conflicts (hourly) and Current events portal (every 30 minutes) add new conflicts and recent developments. Casualty numbers extracted automatically from news text may contain errors and are always labelled as automatic.
  • Live articles: the GDELT Project’s public API lists articles from the last 7 days for each event.
  • Maps: imagery from Esri World Imagery, night lights from NASA Black Marble, elevation from Mapzen Terrarium, borders and places from Natural Earth.

Limits and principles

Casualty figures may include claims by parties to a conflict, and organisations count differently. This site does not support any state or faction; where figures disagree, we show the range together with the sources. If you spot an error, please tell us via the contact page.
